## Setting up LED Strips in Home Assistant using WLED

*January 31, 2025*

Recently we got bids for a kitchen remodel and some included an option to put
under-cabinet lighting, which I think is kinda cool.  They ballparked it at like
$1,000 and who knows if their tech would even be smart enough to integrate with
Home Assistant which to me is mandatory.  Moreover, that price is pretty high.

Around the same time I heard about the
[WLED Project](https://kno.wled.ge/)
so decided to try it out.

Below are the steps I took to get things basically up and running.  There is a
small bit of soldering involved but nothing fancy.  I'm going to link to the
specific components I used as a courtesy in case helpful to you, but there may
well be better options. I'm not an influencer so I get no kickback from the
sources.

## Assembly

I wired a D1 Mini to the power the LED strip as shown in the schematic below but using the D1 mini's 
microUSB to power the whole thing.  It certainly works, but I'm concerned about the power draw from
the LED strip (e.g., when it is on bright) burning out the D1 mini.
So after the initial experiment I decided to go with a legit power supply, as
the 144-LED strip can draw up to 8.6A (144 x 60mA) which is way more than the D1 Mini can provide (without
smoking its voltage regulator), and in any case most power adapters provide only 2-3A.

# Set up with WLED

The following steps will get your D1 Mini set up with WLED. 

1. Download the appropriate version of the LED firmware. For the D1 mini in the parts list above, I used
WLED\_0.15.0\_ESP8266.bin.

2. Plug your D1 mini into a USB port on your laptop or server.

3. Open a web browser to [web.esphome.io](https://web.esphome.io)

4. Hit **Connect** and Select the USB port your D1 Mini is plugged into.

5. Select **Install** and point it to the firmware you downloaded.  This will erase the D1 Mini
and install the WLED firmware.

6. Your D1 Mini will now act as a wifi access point and will start to broadcast its wifi network
(with an SSID  something like WLED AP etc.).  Connect to it (.e.g., from a smartphone if you don't want
to disconnect your laptop from the Internet) and then open a browser (on the same device connected
 to the WLED wifi network) and you should be taken to the D1 Mini's config page. If not, try going to
https://4.3.2.1.  If you are asked for a password, the WLED default is *wled1234*.

7. At the D1 Mini's WLED page, go to the Info page (an "I" icon) and make a note of the Mac address
as this will help you determine its IP address, which you'll need later for integrating the device
with Home Assistant.

8. Stil at the D1 Mini WLED page,  go to *WiFi Settings* and hit *scan* for wifi networks.
Select yours and enter your wifi password.

9. Hit save and restart.  The D1 Mini will reboot and attach to your wifi network (unless you mistyped
the password).
